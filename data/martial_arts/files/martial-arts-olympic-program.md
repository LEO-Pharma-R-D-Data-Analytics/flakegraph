# Combat Sports on the Olympic Programme
Recognition, demonstration, and medal status across selected Games

## Federations and recognition

Olympic participation joins an art to several institutional layers. An international federation maintains sport rules and qualification systems, while the IOC recognizes federations and approves each Games programme. Host organizers then provide venues and schedules. These roles should not be compressed into one relation because recognition, governance, and presentation answer different historical questions.

The judo, karate, and taekwondo federations form repeated bridges in this corpus. Each connects a martial art to the IOC, but their Olympic paths differ. Recognition does not guarantee permanent programme status, and programme inclusion does not transfer ownership of the broader cultural art. The graph records institutional facts while keeping those boundaries visible.

The International Judo Federation is recognized by the International Olympic Committee as judo's international federation.

The World Karate Federation is recognized by the International Olympic Committee as the international federation for sport karate.

World Taekwondo is recognized by the International Olympic Committee as taekwondo's Olympic federation.

## Tokyo as a recurring Olympic setting

Tokyo 1964 gave judo its first Olympic medal programme and tied a Japanese educational art to a major international sporting event. The event node is located in Tokyo, while the art is separately linked to the event. This structure allows a query to move from Kano and the Kodokan through judo to Olympic institutions without implying that one person alone secured programme inclusion.

Karate's appearance at Tokyo 2020 followed a different route and occurred decades later. The Games retained their 2020 title after postponement and were held in 2021. Karate's medal events provided a conspicuous public platform, but inclusion for that edition did not settle its status at all future Games. Precise event naming keeps the corpus conservative.

## Taekwondo from demonstration to medals

Demonstration status and medal status are not synonyms. Taekwondo appeared as a demonstration sport at Seoul 1988, where a Korean host setting made the presentation culturally resonant. The event occurred in the Republic of Korea, but taekwondo's modern organizations and schools already extended beyond national borders. The demonstration is a milestone, not proof of an isolated national history.

At Sydney 2000, taekwondo appeared as an official medal sport. The later event followed the Seoul demonstration by twelve years and represented a different programme status. A PRECEDED edge captures that sequence without claiming that the earlier event caused the later decision by itself. Federation work, IOC votes, and international participation filled the interval.

Taekwondo was presented as a demonstration sport at the Seoul 1988 Olympic Games.

The Seoul 1988 Olympic Games occurred in the Republic of Korea.

Taekwondo was presented as an official medal sport at the Sydney 2000 Olympic Games.

The Sydney 2000 Olympic Games occurred in Australia.

The Seoul 1988 demonstration preceded taekwondo's medal programme at Sydney 2000.

## Boxing and the longer sport programme

Boxing entered the modern Olympic programme much earlier than these martial arts highlighted here. Its appearance at St. Louis 1904 belongs to a period when Olympic formats and participation were still developing. Linking the sport to that named event provides a stable milestone while avoiding the broader claim that all forms of boxing began there.

The event's United States location also creates a geographic bridge to later commission-regulated combat sports. Boxing and MMA do not share one ruleset, yet public authorities and combat-sport commissions often regulate both. The Olympic edge and the regulatory edge thus connect without turning one sport into a direct descendant of the other.

Boxing was presented as an Olympic sport at the St. Louis 1904 Olympic Games.

The St. Louis 1904 Olympic Games occurred in the United States.
